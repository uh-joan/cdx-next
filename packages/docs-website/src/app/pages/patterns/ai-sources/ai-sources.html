An AI answer is only as trustworthy as the user's ability to check it. When the answer is grounded in documents, make every claim traceable: number it with an inline citation that reveals the exact passage and links to the source, and list the ranked documents behind the answer. reg-ai does both; cmc turns source ids into focusable tooltip triggers. It's the 's Trustworthy principle made concrete, and it pairs with the .

Two linked surfaces

  • Inline citations — numbered references in the answer; on hover or focus they reveal the cited passage, a source link, and (for off-language sources) an AI translation with see original.
  • Source list — the ranked documents behind the answer, numbered, with title, publisher, territory and last-updated. Show a handful; collapse the rest.

Do

  • Number claims and reveal the cited passage on hover and focus.
  • Rank the sources, show a few, collapse the rest.
  • Link every citation and document to the real source.
  • Give each document title, source, territory and last-updated.
  • Mark AI translations as AI and offer the original.

Don't

  • Leave claims uncited or citations dead.
  • Use a hover-only citation with no keyboard path.
  • Dump every document with no ranking or collapse.
  • Show translated text without disclosing it's AI.

Rank, preview, collapse

List the documents in relevance order and show a preview; collapse the rest behind "Show N more sources". These thresholds mirror reg-ai.

Example

Hover or focus a citation chip to reveal the cited passage and source link; source 2 is off-language, so it offers an AI translation with "See original". The ranked list shows three, then "Show 3 more sources".
