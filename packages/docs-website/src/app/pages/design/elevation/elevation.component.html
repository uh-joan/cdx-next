Elevation styles are used to create distance and separation between surfaces (i.e. "elevate objects"). Each elevation level produces a three-dimensional appearance to an object by utilizing shadows. As the elevation levels increase, the elevation level's (drop-shadow css) span increases along the X-axis and Y-axis. This increase in elevation level allows the object to achieve a three-dimensional appearance of more depth along the Z-axis.

Elevation is measured from the front of one surface to the front of another, an element's elevation indicates the distance between surfaces and the depth of its shadow.

Elevation Settings

While Material automatically contains the correct settings for each elevation level, it's important to understand how the values are configured.

X-axis:

The offset of the drop shadow along the horizontal plane (X-axis) that controls the top and bottom shadow.

Y-axis:

The offset of the drop shadow along the vertical plane (Y-axis) that controls the direction of the right and left shadows.

Blur

Adjusts the feathering (blur) of the shadow color.

Spread:

Adjusts the size of the shadow to represent the distance between the foreground and background.

Inner shadow:

Inner shadows simulate depths. The shadow will be applied within the layer or object and is contained within the layer's bounds.

Drop shadow:

Drop shadows are applied to the outside of the layer, creating a 3-D illusion.

Angular Material defines 24 levels of elevation styles. For simplicities sake, you obviously do not need to reference all of them. By default, Angular Material components have the appropriate elevation level built into it.

Use elevations:

To distinguish between two levels of elevation:

If there are two different levels of elevation in an experience, raise the elevation level on the highest-priority item to eliminate confusion and differentiate between surfaces.

For overlapping experiences:

Elevation is commonly used with notifications that require user interaction or when backgrounds are the same color but don't offer enough contrast. Adding more depth to objects helps prioritize that action above others.

To distance objects or highlight motion:

For some components, elevation is used for states.

To show visual hierarchy:

The higher an object is along the Z-axis, the more depth it will appear to have, helping users visually prioritize that component being above others. Lowering the Z-axis value makes the component appear flatter.

The FAB overlaps the content on the screen, indicating that it is in front of the content.

Without visual cues of surface edges and overlap, it is not possible to determine how many surfaces are present, which surfaces are sitting above others, nor the degree of elevation between surfaces. The level of importance and separation of actionable elements are not easily identified.

While a modal or dialog can indicate surface overlap, the degree of elevation still needs to be indicated.

The lack of elevation for a modal or dialog can make it difficult to distinguish the dialog from the background.

Avoid using shadows for stylistic purposes only.

Elevation-levels are visual indicators of layering. The only concern is to make sure that the level of element separation is obvious to the user. There is no technical constraint of shadows.
