Color principles

Helix color usage is guided by three core principles:

  • Purposeful – Every color should serve a clear function—communicating meaning, status, or emphasis. It should never be used purely for decoration.
  • Neutral-first – A monochromatic foundation of black, white and greys keeps interfaces clean and allows functional colors to stand out where they’re needed most.
  • Accessible – All color combinations must meet accessibility standards to ensure information is readable and inclusive for all users.

Primary

Helix uses a tone of black as its primary color. It provides strong contrast for text and is used consistently across key elements like primary buttons and headings. This approach ensures clarity, accessibility, and a clean, modern aesthetic—allowing functional colors to stand out where emphasis is needed.

Neutrals

Neutral colors form the backbone of our UI. They create structure, provide contrast, and support content without competing for attention. Use them for backgrounds, text, surfaces and containers.

Accent

Accent is a bold purple used to create high visual emphasis. Use it sparingly - only when something needs to stand out with clear intent. Overuse can reduce impact and lead to visual noise.

AI gradient

The AI Gradient is a distinctive blue-to-purple gradient derived from the Clarivate brand guidelines. It is used to identify AI-powered functionality across Helix, helping users quickly recognise AI features and interactions. Functional

Functional colors communicate status, intent, or system feedback. They should always be used consistently, with clear meaning and strong contrast for accessibility.

Success

Success colors indicate positive outcomes such as confirmations, completions or validated states.

Warning

Warning colors signal caution or potential risk. Use them to draw attention to areas that may require review or user intervention.

Danger

Danger colors are reserved for destructive actions, errors or critical system messages that need immediate attention.

Info

Info colors provide supporting context or additional detail. They should help guide understanding without adding urgency.
